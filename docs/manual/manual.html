Chapter 1

Introduction

SeaQ Snap Layout brings snap layouts to KDE Plasma 6. Drag a window towards the top edge of the screen, a bar with layouts slides down, and you drop the window onto the zone where it should go. Since version 1.7 there are three more ways to snap – each one can be switched on and off on its own.

⇡Drag, drop, doneNo shortcuts to learn – it works with the mouse movement you already know.
▦Your own layoutsSix layouts ready to go, plus a designer for any split you like – up to 16 zones.
◐Fits your desktopSystem, light or dark colors, any accent. The editor uses your color scheme, decoration and language.
⏱Feels rightDistances, delays, slide speed and six motion curves – drag a snapped window out and it gets its old size back.
⇲Screen edgesPush a window against an edge or corner: maximize, halves, quarters – and it fits around windows that are already snapped.
⊟Window magnetWindows stick to each other and to the screen edges while you move or resize them.
⌨Keyboard shortcuts20 actions – halves, quarters, thirds, maximize, center. Ready-made key schemes or your own keys.
⚙Pure KWin scriptNo extra program running in the background. Install from the KDE Store with two clicks.
What is a “zone”?A layout splits the screen into areas, for example two halves or three columns. Each area is a zone. The snap bar shows every layout as a small picture of the screen; each rectangle in it is a zone you can drop a window onto.

Chapter 2

The snap bar at a glance

The snap bar only appears while you move a window. It never takes space on your screen otherwise.

1Handle – a small pill that appears first when the window gets close to the top. It tells you the bar is ready.
2Snap bar – slides down once the cursor reaches the top edge. It always appears on the screen you are working on.
3Layout – a small picture of your screen. The bar shows all layouts marked In bar.
4Zone – one area of a layout. Hover it to select it.
5Highlighted zone – drawn in the accent color. Releasing the mouse now snaps the window here.
6Name line (optional) – shows the layout and zone name, e.g. “Four quarters · Zone 3”.

The landing preview

While a zone is highlighted, a translucent rectangle in the accent color shows on the desktop exactly where the window will land – including gaps and panels. Move away from the bar and the preview disappears.

Installation

SeaQ Snap Layout is a KWin script. It installs into your home folder, needs no administrator rights and adds no background program.

RequirementDetails
DesktopKDE Plasma 6 (KWin 6) – Wayland or X11
DistributionAny: Fedora, Arch, openSUSE, KDE neon, Kubuntu, Debian …
Extra packagesNone – everything it needs ships with Plasma

Option A – KDE Store (recommended)

  1. Open System Settings → Window Management → KWin Scripts.
  2. Click Get New… at the top right, search for SeaQ Snap Layout and click Install.
  3. Tick the checkbox next to SeaQ Snap Layout and click Apply.

Option B – file from GitHub

  1. Download kdesnaplayout-<version>.kwinscript from the Releases page.
  2. In KWin Scripts click Install from File… and choose the file.
  3. Tick SeaQ Snap Layout and click Apply.

Option C – terminal

# install
kpackagetool6 --type KWin/Script --install kdesnaplayout-1.9.0.kwinscript
# update to a newer version
kpackagetool6 --type KWin/Script --upgrade kdesnaplayout-1.9.0.kwinscript
# switch it on
kwriteconfig6 --file kwinrc --group Plugins --key kdesnaplayoutEnabled true
qdbus6 org.kde.KWin /KWin reconfigure
After an update: log out onceKWin keeps a loaded script in memory. After installing a new version, log out and back in so the new files are used. You can check the version on the About & Help page of the settings editor.

Quick start – your first snap

Three movements, about two seconds.

  1. Grab a window by its title bar and hold the left mouse button.
  2. Move it up. About 140 px below the top edge a small handle appears. Keep going until the cursor touches the top edge – the snap bar slides down.
  3. Point at a zone. It lights up in your accent color, and a preview on the desktop shows where the window will land.
  4. Let go. The window fills exactly that zone.

Changed your mind?

Move the cursor away from the bar, or release the window anywhere outside it. Nothing is snapped and the bar slides back up.

Getting the old size back

Grab a snapped window and drag it away – it returns to the size it had before snapping. You can turn this off on the General page.

Which windows can snap?

Normal, resizable windows. Dialogs, fullscreen windows and apps on your exclusion list never show the snap bar.

More than one screen?

The bar opens on the screen under the cursor, and zones fill that screen’s free area – panels are left uncovered.

Smoother snappingPlasma has its own edge actions. SeaQ Snap Layout does the same and more, so turn Plasma’s off: System Settings → Window Management → Screen Edges → untick Maximize and Tile for windows dragged to the edges (names vary slightly between Plasma versions). The editor shows an orange hint as long as they are on.

Opening the settings

A KWin script has no app icon of its own. Its settings are reached through the gear icon in System Settings, which opens a small quick menu.

  1. Open System Settings → Window Management → KWin Scripts and click the gear icon 1 next to SeaQ Snap Layout.
  2. Click Open Snap Layout settings editor 2. The hint “Ready – click OK to open the settings editor.” appears.
  3. Click OK. The settings editor opens within about a second.

Quick settings

3Snap bar colors switches the snap bar between System, Light and Dark without opening the editor. Everything else – appearance, size, animation, behavior and your own layouts – lives in the settings editor.

Why the extra click?System Settings only saves the quick menu when you press OK or Apply. SeaQ Snap Layout notices the saved button press and opens its editor. The button can be used as often as you like.

More ways to snap

Screen edges

Push a window against an edge or a corner of the screen and it fills that part. Works with or without the snap bar.

Edges – the middle of the left or right edge gives a half, the bottom edge the bottom half, the top edge maximizes (or what you choose).

Corners – the outer part of each edge (20 % by default) gives the quarter in that corner.

A small push – the window has to rest against the edge for 60 ms, so passing by does nothing.

Smart fill

The edges always look at what is already snapped. Halves and quarters never cover a snapped window, and the top edge only fills the free space above it.

⬆

Nothing snapped below → the top edge maximizes.

⬆

A quarter sits at the bottom → only the top half is filled.

6.8
More ways to snap

Keyboard shortcuts

Snap the active window with the keyboard. These are normal KDE global shortcuts – you also find them in System Settings → Shortcuts → KWin.

1 2 3 4
1Use keyboard shortcuts – off leaves the keys assigned but they do nothing. The sidebar switch Shortcuts is the same.
2Key scheme – choose a ready-made set and click Apply: all 16 snapping actions get their keys at once (see chapter 7).
3Reset all – gives every action the key of the chosen scheme again.
4One row per action – shows the keys as key caps. The pencil opens the editor; the arrow ↶ appears when a key differs from the scheme and resets it.

Changing a single key

  1. Click the action, e.g. Snap to the left half. An editor opens below it.
  2. Usual combinations – one click picks the same key with the modifiers of every scheme, e.g. Ctrl+Alt+Left, Meta+Left, Meta+Shift+Left.
  3. Your own combination – toggle Meta Ctrl Alt Shift, choose the key from the list (letters, digits, arrows, F1–F12, number block …) and click Set.
  4. Reset to default brings back the key of the chosen scheme, Remove clears the key of that action.

When a key is already in use

SeaQ Snap Layout asks KDE’s shortcut service before it sets a key. If another action already has it, an orange card names the owner – for example “Ctrl+Alt+F is used by Show Calendar”:

  • Take it over – removes the key from the other action and gives it to SeaQ Snap Layout.
  • Take over all – does that for every conflict in the list.
  • Keep them – leaves everything as it is; that action keeps no key.

Troubleshooting & FAQ

The snap bar does not appear.

Check that the script is ticked in KWin Scripts and that Active is on in the editor. Make sure the app is not on your exclusion list and that you drag a normal, resizable window by its title bar. If Active width along the top edge is below 100 %, move towards the middle of the top edge.

Two things happen when I drag a window to an edge.

Plasma’s own edge actions or snap zones are still active. Switch on Pause KDE's edge tiling and its preview (Screen edges page) and Pause KDE's snap zones while the magnet is on (Window magnet page) – both are on by default.

A keyboard shortcut does nothing.

Check the Shortcuts switch and that the key is shown on the page. If another program grabs the key first, choose a different scheme or key.

I installed an update, but the old version is still running.

KWin keeps loaded scripts in memory. Log out and back in once. The About & Help page shows the running version.

The quick menu button does nothing.

After Open Snap Layout settings editor you have to click OK (or Apply). The editor appears about one second later.

“Could not locate package metadata” in KWin Scripts.

The script folder was removed while KWin still listed it. Install the .kwinscript file again, then log out and back in.

Can I use it with several monitors?

Yes. The bar opens on the screen under the cursor and zones are calculated for that screen.

Something is still wrong – how do I report it?

Collect the log and open an issue on GitHub:

journalctl --user -b | grep kdesnaplayout | tail -n 30

Files, uninstalling & license

Where things are stored

PathContents
~/.config/kde-snaplayout.iniAll settings of the editor and your layouts. Copy it to move your setup to another PC.
~/.config/kglobalshortcutsrc → [kwin]The keys of the shortcuts (managed by KDE).
~/.config/kwinrc → [Script-kdesnaplayout]The two quick-menu values (editor button, snap bar colors).
~/.local/share/kwin/scripts/kdesnaplayoutThe installed script.
PrivacySeaQ Snap Layout works completely offline. It sends no data anywhere and only reads window positions while you drag a window.

Uninstalling

In KWin Scripts, click the trash icon next to SeaQ Snap Layout and then Apply. Or in a terminal:

kpackagetool6 --type KWin/Script --remove kdesnaplayout
rm ~/.config/kde-snaplayout.ini        # optional: delete your settings
